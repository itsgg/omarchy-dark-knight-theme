# Design notes

Why each part of the theme is the way it is. The [README](../README.md) is the
short version.

## Where the colours come from

Not from a design system and not by eye. `src/palette.py` takes six
measurements of one photograph and derives every value from them. Quantised to
sixteen buckets that photograph is monochromatic: every colour with any real
area sits at hue 203 in HLS and 249 in Lab, saturation falls from 34% in the
shadows to 10% at the highlight, chroma never passes 13, and warm pixels are
0.07% of the frame. So the theme is built the same way: every structural
colour is that hue with the image's own saturation for its lightness, and
hierarchy is carried by lightness alone.

The photograph is not in the repository. The six numbers are recorded in
`palette.py` as `MEASURED`, and that is all the script needs: given no
argument it uses them, given an image it measures that one instead.

| Key | Value | How it is decided |
|---|---|---|
| `background` | `#0A1014` | the image's hue at 6% lightness, with the image's own saturation for that lightness |
| `line` | `#283943` | the same ramp at 21%: rules, indent guides, the inactive border |
| `muted` | `#687F8D` | raised until it clears 4.5:1 on the base, because it is every comment and line number, and 3:1 on every raised surface |
| `dark_foreground` | `#86969F` | raised until it clears 4.5:1 on all of them |
| `foreground` | `#CCCFD1` | the ramp above anything in the photograph, because text sits on top of it |
| `accent` | `#99C1DC` | the image's Lab hue at L76, with the least chroma (19) that stands CIE76 16 off every step of the text ramp |
| `active_border` | `#739BB5` | the accent's colour at L62: 6.5:1 on the ground, under the text |
| `accent_dim`, `selection` | `#596D7B`, `#222E36` | steel, the image's own chroma (11): the fills |
| `red` `green` `yellow` `blue` `magenta` `cyan` | one chroma, one lightness | fixed hues, all at 1.75x the image's peak chroma (23), the most colourful anything here is |

Chroma, not saturation. An earlier version equalised the ANSI set on HLS
saturation and green, magenta and cyan came out reading twice as colourful as
blue at the same number, so the terminal looked like a different theme from the
interface around it.

### The accent

Steel, the image's hue at the image's own chroma, is what fills: the selection
and the dim accent. It cannot be the accent as well. At that chroma it sits
CIE76 7 from `light_foreground` and 7 from `dark_foreground`, so focus, the
selected row and a link would be three greys among the text greys.

So the accent is carried by light, like everything else. It sits at L76 with
only as much chroma as it takes to stand CIE76 16 off every step of the text
ramp, found by the script rather than written down, and never more than the
ANSI set.

The window border is the same colour lower down, at L62: 6.5:1 on the ground,
unmistakable beside the inactive one (the audit holds them CIE76 30 apart) and
darker than body text, so the frame never outranks what it frames. A near-white
border, which works on themes with a lighter ground, comes out at 14:1 on this
one, above the text's 12:1. Focus is one solid line in one colour: no glow, no
gradient.

One frame, everywhere. Windows, the menu, the launcher, notifications,
flyouts, tooltips and the polkit card all wear the active border by reference
(`hyprland.active-border`), so nothing carries a hex of its own to drift.

There is one warm mark: the bar's active workspace is `red`, because it is the
one thing on the bar that is looked for rather than read.

The script audits its own output against every floor above and exits non-zero
if one is missed, so a palette generated from a different image either meets
them or says which it did not.

## The shell

`shell.controls.toml`, `shell.bar.toml`, `shell.launcher.toml`,
`shell.menu.toml` and `shell.lock.toml` are section overrides, generated by `src/controls.py`.
Section files rather than a whole `shell.toml`, because Omarchy replaces what
a theme ships wholesale and a section is the smallest freeze available. Focus
is the accent outlined and selection the accent filled, so the two still say
different things. The bar and the pickers are slightly translucent. The theme
sets no blur and no shadow of its own and ships no `hyprland.lua`: Omarchy's
generated one carries the two border colours, and whether windows blur or cast
shadows stays whatever your own Hyprland config says. The lock screen's backdrop is not a theme's to set (Omarchy blurs
the current wallpaper to a soft field); what the theme sets is the input card,
which rests on the dim accent and lights to the active border when you type.

`btop.theme` (`src/btop.py`) puts the monitor on the same rule: a meter is a
ramp of lightness from steel to the accent to the brightest text, and hue is
kept for temperature and for the top of a meter that is running out.

## Neovim and VS Code

Both editors come from `colors.toml` through Omarchy's own templates, so they
match the rest of the theme exactly rather than approximately. The theme used
to ship `neovim.lua` and `vscode.json` pointing both at Kanagawa, chosen
because it is "warm gold on near-black, the same relationship as `--gold-500`
on `--ink-900`". That relationship no longer exists, and both files went.

`neovim.lua` is back, as two overrides rather than another colorscheme. The
first is the indent guides.
Omarchy's template hands aether the palette and stops there, and aether spends
`muted` on text and on decoration alike: comments and line numbers, but also
`NonText`, which is what snacks.nvim draws every indent guide with, and
`IblIndent`, which is indent-blankline's. So the guides arrive at 4.57:1 on
the ground, in the comment colour and at comment weight, on every indented
line of the file. The scope guide arrives louder still, at full `cyan` (6.45:1
under snacks) or `blue` (6.45:1 under indent-blankline): the weight of code,
for a piece of chrome. Dimming `muted` cannot fix that, because it is also the
comment colour and comments are text. So `src/neovim.py` reads Omarchy's
template, resolves it from `colors.toml`, and inserts an `on_highlights` block
that puts the guides on `line` (1.60:1, already this theme's structure colour
and its inactive window border) and the scope guides on `accent_dim` (3.55:1).
Comments, line numbers and every other use of `muted` are untouched.

The second is syntax. aether hands six syntax classes six ANSI hues, and this
palette places the ANSI set at one chroma and one lightness on purpose, so in
the editor every word weighed the same and nothing led the eye. The theme's
rule is one hue carried by light, and `src/neovim.py` applies it to code: hue
is kept for data (strings green, every literal orange, types the accent,
builtins cyan), grammar goes onto the grey ramp below the code it organises
(keywords, conditionals, imports at `light_foreground`), names and calls are
ordinary text, and a definition is the brightest word on its line, in bold.
Under an LSP the same holds: semantic tokens link calls through `Function`,
which is plain, and declarations are raised by their `declaration` modifier.
Docstrings are prose rather than data, so they sit one step above a comment
on the grey ramp; aether gives them a yellow of their own, and in a file that
documents itself that made the documentation the loudest thing on the screen.

Shipping the file freezes the rest of the spec the way `shell.controls.toml`
freezes its section: Omarchy does not overwrite a file the theme ships, so a
change to `neovim.lua.tpl` upstream arrives here only when `src/render.sh` is
run again.

Which install gets the file is the staging rule above: a working copy does, a
clone in `~/.config/omarchy/themes` does not, because Omarchy stages no `.lua`
from one. Nothing in `colors.toml` can stand in for it either, since the whole
problem is one palette key spent on text and on decoration at once and only
code can tell those apart. A clone install is not stuck, though: the same
override in your own Neovim config does the same work, and is under
[docs/INSTALL.md](INSTALL.md#keeping-the-clone-install-and-dimming-the-guides-from-neovim). Fixing it for everyone
at once would mean fixing it a level up, in aether or in `neovim.lua.tpl`,
where it would reach every theme that takes Omarchy's generated spec rather
than this one alone.

## Corners

The theme sets no rounding; Hyprland owns that, in
`~/.config/hypr/looknfeel.lua`, and the shell follows it on its own
(`Commons/Style.qml`: `cornerRadius` mirrors `decoration:rounding`), so the
bar, menu, notifications and OSD match whatever the windows do. It is drawn
for square or nearly square corners, 0 to 4: nearly every window on this
desktop is a terminal, which is a monospace grid, and the border is a line.

**The shell does not watch `looknfeel.lua`**: it re-polls Hyprland at startup,
when a gaps toggle file changes, and when a theme is applied. So after
changing the value the windows change immediately and the panels keep their
old corners until something triggers that poll, and `omarchy-restart-shell` is
the reliable one. Do not use `omarchy-refresh-shell` for this: it resets
`shell.json` to defaults and drops any bar plugins.

GTK is separate again. libadwaita rounds its widgets from its own stylesheet
and Hyprland cannot reach inside a window, so `gtk.css` carries a blanket
`border-radius: 0`, with circular controls left circular because a squared-off
avatar is a different widget rather than a sharper corner.

## GTK and icons

Omarchy generates seventeen surfaces from `colors.toml`, and GTK is not one of
them: `omarchy-theme-set-gnome` only flips Adwaita between light and dark and
sets the icon theme. Every GTK application therefore falls back to stock
Adwaita-dark, which here means Nautilus, its previewer and the file-chooser
portal that every application opens.

`gtk.css` fills that in, generated from the palette by `src/gtk.py`. It names
libadwaita's semantic colours explicitly rather than only the accent, which is
what stops a stock blue turning up in a selection or a link, and it maps the
theme's ramp onto depth: windows on the base, the file view recessed below it,
sidebars, cards and popovers raised above it.

**They need two symlinks to do anything.** Nothing in Omarchy copies a theme's
`gtk.css` anywhere GTK looks, so on their own the files are inert.
`hooks/gtk-follow-theme` makes the links and restarts the portal; installing
it is one line, in [INSTALL.md](INSTALL.md#gtk).

Two files rather than one, because GTK3 does not know `alertdialog`, `banner`,
`toast` or `menubutton`, and it is still the toolkit behind
`xdg-desktop-portal-gtk`, which draws the file chooser every application opens.
That chooser was the last surface wearing stock Adwaita. Measured after the
GTK3 file landed: header `#0B1116`, sidebar `#0A1115`, list `#0D1317`, which
are this theme's grounds rather than Adwaita's `#353535` and `#2d2d2d`.

`xdg-desktop-portal-gtk` reads its stylesheet once at start, which is why the
hook restarts it on every theme switch.

Pointing at `current/theme` rather than at this theme means it follows theme
switches on its own, and any theme shipping a `gtk.css` gets picked up too.

`icons.theme` is `Yaru-prussiangreen-dark`, which `omarchy-theme-set-gnome`
applies on its own. It is chosen by measurement rather than by name: its folder
colour is `#6BADAA` at chroma 22.3, within a point of the 23 this theme
gives its ANSI set, which is the most colourful anything here is allowed to
be. `Yaru-blue-dark` matches the accent's hue more closely but its folders are
`#5AA8FD` at chroma 49.4, more than twice that ceiling, and with an icon on
every row that is the loudest thing in a window.

## Background

One, generated by `src/flat.py`, no raster source at all: `1-origin.png`. The
emblem centred where two accent axes cross, on a steel hairline grid that runs
out into the ground through a mask. It is the boot screen at full size.

No blur filters, no gradient fills, every edge a real vector edge on a flat
ground, saved as lossless PNG so a one pixel hairline stays one pixel wide.
The one soft thing is the grid, whose lines fade out through a mask; a fade on
a hairline cannot band.

The ground is flat on purpose. A radial vignette on a near-black ground spans
only a few dozen grey levels across half the frame, which shows as concentric
rings on an 8-bit panel, and dithering does not survive JPEG. A flat ground
has nothing to band, needs no dither, and keeps the file small (under 100 KB). The hairlines are strong enough to be seen on a real panel and far
below a window's text, and the emblem is filled solid before it is tinted, so
the axes stop at the mark instead of running through it. A wallpaper competes
with your windows for the same attention and loses if it tries to be the
subject, so this is built to sit behind a terminal all day.

`omarchy-theme-set` sorts the backgrounds and takes the first, so anything you
drop in `~/.config/omarchy/backgrounds/dark-knight/` sorts ahead of this one:
the sort is over full paths.

### The emblem

`src/bat.py` holds it as cubic beziers. The right half is taken from a
reference vector (Wikimedia Commons, "Batman logo (solid black
silhouette).svg"), normalised about its own axis, and the left half is that
path mirrored, so the outline is exactly symmetric and is one closed path with
no seam, which matters the moment it is stroked rather than filled. It is the
modern emblem.

### The preview

`preview.png` is what Omarchy's theme switcher shows beside every other
theme's, and the stock previews are all one thing: a real desktop with Neovim,
btop, a terminal listing and Files tiled in the same four places. So this one
is that too, a screenshot taken by `src/preview.sh` with everything pointed at
this repository, and the carousel compares like with like.

### Plymouth

`unlock.png` is the wallpaper's origin cut out of it: the same grid, the same
two axes and the emblem where they cross, on a transparent ground, with the
grid fading out toward the edge of the image so the patch does not end in a
hard rectangle. Plymouth paints the ground from `colors.toml` and centres the
image, so the boot screen and the desktop are the same picture.
